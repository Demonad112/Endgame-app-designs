How the analysis works

The Character page reads poe.ninja’s data and ranks what to fix. This page explains what is read as given, what is worked out here and what is left unguessed.

How the grade is formed

The grade has two equal halves, defence and damage, each worth up to 0.5. Keystones are applied first because they can change a verdict: a Chaos Inoculation build is not “short on chaos resistance”.

The defensive half adds up:

0.30
Combined pool (life + energy shield + ward) above 6,000. Between 4,000 and 6,000 earns 0.15.
0.15
All three elemental resistances at the character’s own maximum, as poe.ninja reports it.
0.05
Chaos resistance at or above 30 %, or immunity to chaos damage.

Damage compares your best hitting skill with the ladder for your league and ascendancy. It scores 0.50 at the 75th percentile or above, 0.40 from the median up, 0.28 from the 25th percentile up and 0.15 below that.

0.50 to 0.75

0.30 to 0.50

below 0.30

When damage can’t be graded (no ladder sample or no hitting skill), the score runs from the defence half alone to that plus 0.5, and the grade shows as a range such as D–B. Guessing would invent a damage score, and scoring zero would punish the build for missing data.

The pool and resistance bands are this project’s judgement, not game constants.

Largest hit

Survival is led by the smallest hit that kills you. poe.ninja reports the biggest hit you survive for each damage type, and the lowest one is the headline.

Any type below 40 % of your best maximum hit is named as a one-shot risk, not just the lowest.

For a hit mitigated only by resistance, the largest survivable hit is pool / (1 − resistance). The app uses this in two labelled places: to show the resistance poe.ninja’s maximum hits imply, when the physical maximum hit equals your life and energy shield pool; and in the What-if resistance sliders, which rescale the maximum hit as maxHit × (1 − effective) / (1 − effective′), with resistance clamped to its cap.

Why EHP overstates safety

Effective health pool is poe.ninja’s averaged pool. It is reported, and drawn on the maximum-hit chart as a dashed line, but never led with.

An average hides the damage type you have no answer to: the reference character reads 13,569 EHP, yet a 3,808 chaos hit kills it. When EHP overstates the smallest killing hit by more than 1.5×, the verdict says so.

How findings are ranked

Findings are ranked by gain for cost, so a free fix beats an equal-sized fix that costs currency.

A cost with an amount multiplies the weight. A finding with no measured gain ranks last among actionable ones.

Every number comes from the imported data. Anything that can’t be confirmed is listed under “Could not be determined”, and findings a keystone rules out are listed separately.

The optional Mapping and Bossing views reorder the same list: Mapping weights damage and efficiency × 1.25 and survivability × 0.85; Bossing weights survivability × 1.25, damage × 1.1 and efficiency × 0.85. The default view applies no weighting.

Where a number came from

Every figure carries one of five labels, and one kind is never shown as another:

Observed
Read as given from poe.ninja or the Path of Building export it attaches. Not recomputed here.
PoB-simulated
Measured by running Path of Building. Kept apart from observed values.
Derived
Arithmetic done here on observed values. Check it against the source before relying on it.
Nominal
What the game data says it grants in isolation. Not measured on this build.
Unknown
The data cannot establish this value. A missing label is unknown, never observed.

Where Path of Building or the app’s formulas disagree with poe.ninja, it is flagged: within 0.5 % is a match, within 5 % (or one unit of rounding) is minor, and anything larger is major.

Reference content on the Checklist and Atlas pages is flagged Unverified or Conflicting sources when its source is not confirmed; verify those in-game.

Read from poe.ninja, computed here

Read from poe.ninja: defences (maximum hits, resistances, pool, effective health pool) and per-skill damage. The damage module only reads and labels these figures.

Computed here: the grade, the finding order, implied resistances, What-if projections and the ladder comparison. The app’s own formulas are only used to project and to cross-check poe.ninja.

Ladder sample: poe.ninja’s builds search gives the top 100 rows, nearly all level 100. It can say “the best builds of your class run X”, but never “you are in the Nth percentile”.

Character data over 24 hours old is marked out of date, since newer gear changes won’t show. The browser can’t call poe.ninja directly, so reads go through this project’s proxy.

How the farm planner scores a map

The Dashboard’s farm planner scores a map, not a drop. It adds what the tablets and Waystone give, weights each stat for your farm, subtracts the danger and reports three figures:

Reward
Σ (stat total × the preset’s weight for that stat). A stat with no weight in the preset adds nothing.
Danger
Σ (stat total × the stat’s danger rating). Only stats the data rates as hazards count.
Net
Reward − Danger × the danger setting: 1.5 for Play safe, 1 for Balanced, 0.5 for Greedy.

Totals add by default, “less” stats multiply and flags count. Rolls scale with the Atlas passives you tick, and changes show as a percentage of the starting figure (“new” when that is 0).

Weights and danger ratings (0 to 3 per defence layer) come from a fact-check of patch 0.5.5 and are relative judgements; the footnote counts the unverified ones. With “Danger for my build” on, the rating averages the layers your last imported character relies on.

A farm tile reads “Fits your build” or “Harder for your build” only when that farm has a saved loadout with danger mods and a character is imported. The rule is this app’s own and is labelled unverified.

Rules come from the data: up to 3 tablets per map (4 with overlapping towers or a City map), and a Waystone takes 6 mods (10 once corrupted). Spawn weights aren’t available, so roll odds aren’t modelled.

It is a ranking aid. The danger scale is not calibrated against run logs yet, so treat a large Net swing from a single danger stat with suspicion.

What is deliberately not guessed

No DPS thresholds. Damage is only compared with figures seen on the ladder, never made-up targets. Without a sample, the damage half stays unscored.

Missing is not zero. A resistance that wasn’t reported is unknown, neither credited nor listed as short. Incomplete pools aren’t graded, and a missing update time reads “unknown”.

Unconfirmed keystones are not applied. A keystone that is allocated but whose effect is not corroborated is named in the caveats, and its corrections are left out.

Stated rather than guessed — a wrong number is worse than a missing one.
